# Which Geimori Grinder Should You Buy? GU38 vs GU63 vs T38 vs GU64

A practical buying guide built from real search behavior, reviewer feedback, and customer ratings.

## Start With Your Coffee Workflow, Not the Spec Sheet

People don't buy grinders because they want a motor — they buy them because they want a better cup. That's why the most common question we hear is simple: "Which one is right for me?"

The short answer is that every grinder in the Geimori line exists for a different workflow. Once you know what you brew, how often, and where, the choice becomes clear.

## GU38: The Budget Single-Dose Workhorse

If you're just moving away from pre-ground coffee, the GU38 is the most forgiving place to start. It's a true single-dose grinder: you weigh your beans, grind what you need, and get almost nothing left behind.

Reviewers consistently describe it as "budget friendly" without the usual budget compromises. Verified customers rate it 4.2/5, praising its consistent grind for both pour-over and espresso.

## GU63: The One That Does Espresso and Filter Well

The GU63 is the model our customers buy when they want to dial in espresso at home but don't want to spend $500+. It has a stepless adjustment range wide enough to jump between espresso and French press in seconds.

Verified buyers give it 4.4/5 on average, and YouTube reviewers have called it "the budget espresso grinder I'd actually recommend" and a direct threat to grinders costing twice as much.

One real-world tip from our review data: use the included bellows after grinding — a few pumps clears most retained grounds and keeps your next dose fresh.

## T38 / T38 Plus: Precision Where There's No Outlet

The T38 is the cordless option for people who brew at work, on the trail, or anywhere without a counter and a plug. The T38 Plus adds a plug-in option with the same burr geometry.

Verified customers rate the T38 battery model 4.5/5, and reviewers have highlighted it as an "amazing quality camping coffee grinder" — consistent grinding in a package that fits in a travel bag.

## GU64: Flat-Burr Clarity Without the Pro Price

Flat burrs produce the clean, separated flavors that specialty coffee drinkers look for, especially in filter brewing. The GU64 brings a 64mm flat burr to the sub-$500 bracket, where most alternatives sit at $800+.

Reviewers have framed it as "the best 64mm flat burr grinder under $500?" — and the question mark disappears once you see the particle distribution tests.

## A Simple Decision Rule

- Espresso only, tight budget → GU38
- Espresso + filter, want room to grow → GU63
- No outlet nearby, or travel often → T38 / T38 Plus
- Flat-burr clarity for specialty coffee → GU64
- Not sure? Ask yourself what you brew most often this week — that's your answer.

## Bottom Line

There's no "best grinder" — only the best grinder for your workflow. Match the machine to the coffee you actually drink, and you'll get more out of every bag of beans.
