# Home Espresso Troubleshooting: Fix the 5 Most Common Problems

The issues customers and viewers ask about most — and the step-by-step fixes that actually work.

## 1. Your Grind Setting Drifts or Feels Stuck

"My grind dial moved by itself" and "the dial won't turn" are two of the most common support topics we see in reviews and comments.

The fix: recalibrate and lock the collar. Most stepped grinders have a calibrated zero point — turn the dial to its limit, reset the marker to zero, then adjust from there. If it feels stuck, a small amount of coffee residue in the threads is usually the culprit: brush the collar, not just the burrs.

After calibration, always set your grind with the motor running or the burrs empty, and tighten any locking ring before pulling a shot.

## 2. Too Much Ground Coffee Left Behind (Retention)

A few tenths of a gram stuck in the burr chamber is normal, especially with single-dose grinding. The fastest fix is the one in the box: pump the bellows 3-4 times after grinding. Verified customers confirm it clears most of the retained grounds.

If retention becomes excessive, grind a few grams of cheap beans through to "self-clean" the burrs, then brush the chute. You should see retention drop back to normal.

## 3. Your Shot Is Sour or Bitter — Dial In, Don't Give Up

Sour usually means under-extracted: grind finer, or increase your dose slightly. Bitter usually means over-extracted: grind coarser, or pull a shorter shot.

Change one variable at a time, one step at a time, and keep notes. In 2-3 attempts you'll normally hit the sweet spot — that's exactly how baristas do it.

Also check temperature: if your espresso machine's brew temperature has drifted low, output can taste weak and watery. Run a flush cycle and descale if you see scale buildup — low temperature is a classic descaling symptom.

## 4. Steam Wand Problems: Weak Froth or No Steam

A steam wand that produces weak foam is almost always blocked milk residue, not a broken machine.

Fix it: after every session, purge the wand for a second, then wipe it with a damp cloth. Once a week, soak the tip in hot water or run a steam purge with the wand submerged. If steam stops entirely, descale the boiler — mineral scale is the #1 cause of steam failure.

For richer microfoam, start with cold milk, keep the tip just below the surface, and stop the moment the pitcher feels warm to the touch.

## 5. Flavors Lingering After Switching Beans

Switching from a dark roast to a light one and tasting yesterday's coffee? Grind a 3-5 gram purge dose of the new beans through, then brush the burr chamber. For stubborn oils, use grinder cleaning tablets once a month.

This also keeps your grinder ready for different brew methods — espresso in the morning, pour-over in the afternoon — without flavor crossover.
