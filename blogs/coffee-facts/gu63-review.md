# 0.1g or 2g?
What a GU63 Retention Test
Really Tells Us
Same grinder. One small change.
A very different result.
01 The Surprise
## The bellows made a big difference.
In a recent Coffeeness review, the GU63 showed a maximum input-to-output difference of **0.1g with bellows.** Without using them, the reviewer reported **roughly 2g** left behind.

02 Where Does the Coffee Go?
## Not all retention is the same.
A near-perfect dose weight doesn't necessarily mean there's no old coffee left inside. These are three different things worth separating.
1. Dose Difference
Input versus output weight for one dose.
2. Grounds Left
Coffee remaining in the grinder after grinding.
3. Exchange Retention
Old coffee mixed into the next dose.
Concept diagram — not a view of the GU63 interior or measured particle distribution.

For V60 and Chemex, cups were enjoyable, although lighter roasts showed more fines and reduced clarity.

05 The Big Question
## Does low retention count if you have to use the bellows?
The GU63 achieved a very small dose-weight difference with bellows. Whether that counts as “low retention” depends on how you define it — and how you use your grinder every morning.
06 The Bottom Line
## The numbers tell one story. Your routine tells the rest.
Coffeeness's review offers unusually useful detail: sweet espresso, good filter coffee, and a workflow where bellows really matter.
