01 · Extraction
## Grind size changes the way coffee extracts
Coffee extraction is shaped by several variables — including grind size, dose, water, time, temperature, and brewing method.
Finer grind
### More surface area
- Usually increases extraction
- Often slows water flow
- Small changes can have a noticeable effect
Coarser grind
### Less surface area
- Usually decreases extraction
- Often allows faster water flow
- Can reduce over-extraction
**If your coffee tastes sharp, sour, or underdeveloped:** try a slightly finer grind, or allow more extraction.
**If it tastes dry, bitter, or overly heavy:** try a slightly coarser grind, or reduce extraction.
The goal is not one “perfect” setting. Start with a reasonable range, brew, taste, and adjust one variable at a time.
Use these recommendations as starting points, not fixed rules.

03 · Dial in
## A repeatable dial-in workflow
The easiest way to learn from each brew is to keep most variables stable and change only one thing at a time.
01
### Set a baseline
Choose a reasonable grind range for your brew method. Use a familiar recipe if possible.
02
### Brew consistently
Keep coffee, dose, water, temperature, and technique as stable as you can.
03
### Taste & observe
Notice flow, brew time, clarity, sweetness, sharpness, bitterness, and body.
04
### Make one change
Adjust grind slightly, brew again, and compare the new result with the last cup.
**Sharp / sour**Often points toward more extraction. A slightly finer grind may help.
**Dry / bitter**Often points toward less extraction. A slightly coarser grind may help.
**Weak / thin**Check grind, dose, and overall extraction before making large changes.
**Heavy / muddy**Try reducing fines, coarsening slightly, or reviewing brew time and agitation.

04 · Workflow
## Grind quality is more than grind size
Consistency
### Make comparison easier
When particle sizes vary widely, different particles can extract at different rates. A more controlled distribution can make recipes easier to compare and repeat.
Static
### Keep the workflow cleaner
Static can make grounds cling to surfaces, scatter around the workspace, or complicate dosing.
Retention
### Improve dose control
Coffee left inside the grinder can affect dose accuracy, workflow consistency, and switching between coffees.
Maintenance
### Protect consistency over time
A grinder that is easier to clean and maintain is easier to keep predictable in daily use.
![Geimori grinder in a quiet pour-over coffee setup](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0569/8247/0796/files/geimori_pour_over_lifestyle.webp?v=1788944009)
Workflow → flavor
### Good equipment should make the process easier to repeat.
A cleaner, more controlled grinding workflow helps you focus on the variables that matter in the cup instead of fighting static, retention, or inconsistent adjustment.
Choose by workflow as much as specifications.
