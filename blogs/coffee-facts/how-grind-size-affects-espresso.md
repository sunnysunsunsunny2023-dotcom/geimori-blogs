# Espresso Grind Size Explained: How Fine Should You Grind?
Grind size is one of the most powerful variables in espresso. A small change can noticeably alter how fast water moves through the coffee bed, how long the shot takes, and how the espresso tastes.
**Quick answer:** Espresso usually needs a fine grind, but there is no single universal setting. If your shot runs too fast, grind finer. If it runs too slowly or barely flows, grind coarser. Keep the dose and other variables as consistent as possible while adjusting.
## What Grind Size Should You Use for Espresso?
Espresso generally requires a fine grind because the brewing time is short and the coffee bed needs enough resistance to control water flow. However, "fine" is only a starting point. The correct setting depends on the coffee, dose, basket, grinder, machine, and target recipe.
Instead of looking for one perfect grind number, use shot behavior and taste to guide your adjustments.
**A useful starting point:** Many espresso recipes begin around a 1:2 brew ratio, such as 18 g of dry coffee producing about 36 g of espresso. A shot time around 25 to 35 seconds can be a useful diagnostic range, but taste matters more than hitting an exact number.
## How Grind Size Changes Espresso Flow
### Finer Grind
Finer particles create more resistance in the coffee bed, so water usually moves through more slowly. This can increase contact time and change extraction.
### Coarser Grind
Coarser particles generally create less resistance, allowing water to pass through faster. If the grind is too coarse for the recipe, the shot may run quickly and taste thin or sharp.
Grind size is not the only variable that affects flow. Dose, puck preparation, basket geometry, pressure, roast level, and the coffee itself can also change shot behavior.
## How to Tell If Your Espresso Grind Is Too Fine or Too Coarse
### Shot runs too fast
Try grinding finer. Also check dose, distribution, and whether channeling is causing water to find an easy path through the puck.
### Shot runs too slowly
Try grinding coarser. If the machine is barely producing espresso, the grind may be restricting flow too much.
### Espresso tastes sour or thin
A finer grind may help increase extraction, but sourness can also come from low brew temperature, an unsuitable recipe, or uneven extraction.
### Espresso tastes harsh or dry
A coarser grind may help, but bitterness and dryness are not caused by grind size alone. Roast level, temperature, ratio, and channeling also matter.
## How to Dial In Espresso Grind Size
1. **Choose a dose and keep it constant.** For example, use 18.0 g for each test shot.
1. **Choose a target yield.** A 1:2 ratio is a practical place to start, such as 18 g in and 36 g out.
1. **Pull a shot and observe the flow.** Record shot time, yield, and taste.
1. **Adjust only the grind first.** Go finer if the shot is clearly too fast, or coarser if it is clearly too slow.
1. **Make small changes.** Espresso can react strongly to very small grind adjustments.
1. **Taste again.** Use time as a diagnostic tool, not as the final goal.
## Why Small Grind Changes Matter So Much in Espresso
Espresso uses a compact coffee bed and a short brew time. Because of that, even a small change in particle size can noticeably alter resistance and flow.
This is why stepless or finely stepped grinders can be useful for espresso. Smaller adjustment increments make it easier to move between "too fast" and "too slow" without overshooting the useful range.
## Does Grind Size Affect Extraction Time?
Yes. In most espresso setups, grinding finer increases resistance and tends to slow the shot, while grinding coarser reduces resistance and tends to speed it up.
But extraction time should not be treated as a fixed quality score. Two coffees can taste best at different shot times, and uneven puck preparation can create fast flow even when the grind is very fine.
## Why Espresso Can Still Run Fast Even With a Fine Grind
If the grind is already fine but the shot still runs quickly, do not automatically keep grinding finer. Check for:
- Uneven distribution
- Channeling
- An underfilled basket
- Poor puck preparation
- A coffee that needs a different dose or recipe
- Large changes in bean age or roast style
Grind size controls resistance, but the coffee bed must also offer that resistance evenly.
## How Roast Level Can Change Your Grind Setting
Different coffees may need different grind settings even when you keep the same dose and yield. Bean density, roast development, age, and processing can all change how a coffee grinds and extracts.
This is normal. Dialing in is not about finding one permanent grinder setting. It is about adjusting the grind to the coffee and recipe you are using now.
## Does Burr Type Matter for Espresso Grind Size?
Burr geometry can influence particle distribution, workflow, and how a grinder responds to adjustments, but grind size still needs to be dialed in for the specific coffee and recipe.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Espresso Grind Size
### Should espresso be ground fine or coarse?
Espresso is usually ground fine. The exact setting should be adjusted until the shot flows at a useful rate and tastes balanced for your coffee and recipe.
### What happens if espresso is ground too fine?
The shot may run very slowly or stall because the coffee bed creates too much resistance. Taste can also become harsh, dry, or unbalanced, although flavor depends on more than grind size alone.
### What happens if espresso is ground too coarse?
Water can pass through the puck too quickly, often producing a thin or underdeveloped shot. Grinding finer is usually the first adjustment to try.
### If espresso runs too fast, should I grind finer?
Usually yes. A finer grind increases resistance and tends to slow the shot. Also check distribution and channeling if the shot remains unusually fast.
### If espresso runs too slow, should I grind coarser?
Usually yes. A coarser grind reduces resistance and tends to increase flow. Make small adjustments rather than making a large jump.
### Is 25 to 30 seconds the perfect espresso shot time?
No single shot time is perfect for every coffee. A range around 25 to 35 seconds can be a useful starting reference, but taste, yield, and consistency should guide the final recipe.
## The Bottom Line
Espresso grind size controls how much resistance the coffee bed creates and is one of the fastest ways to change shot flow. If a shot runs too fast, go finer. If it runs too slowly, go coarser. Keep other variables stable, make small adjustments, and use taste to decide when the shot is truly dialed in.
