If you've invested in an espresso machine, congratulations—you’re one step closer to making café-quality coffee at home. But there’s one critical factor that can make or break your espresso: the coffee beans you use. Choosing the right beans isn’t just about taste—it’s also about compatibility with your machine, your grind size, and your personal brewing goals.
In this blog, we’ll guide you through the key considerations when selecting coffee beans for espresso, whether you're a beginner or a seasoned home barista.
Let’s dive in!☕
### 1.Choose Freshly Roasted Beans (and Know the Roast Date)
Freshness is everything when it comes to espresso. Ideally, you should use beans 7–21 days after roasting.
Beans that are too fresh (under 5 days) release too much CO₂, causing uneven extraction and excessive crema.
Pre-ground coffee loses freshness in minutes or beans that are more than a month old may taste flat, sour, or stale.
**Tip:** Look for coffee bags with a roast date, not just an expiration date.
**Storage:** Keep beans in an airtight container, away from light & heat.
### 2.What’s the Best Roast for Espresso?
While espresso can technically be made with any roast level, medium to dark roasts are most commonly used. These roasts deliver the rich body, deep flavor, and syrupy texture mouthfeel.  These roasts also extract more evenly under the pressure of an espresso machine, leading to a better-balanced shot.
**Medium-Dark to Dark Roast** - Best for traditional espresso, with chocolatey, nutty, and caramelized notes.
**Medium Roast** - Works well for fruitier or brighter espresso (common in specialty coffee).
**Light Roasts** - Too acidic for espresso unless you enjoy bright, fruity shots.
### 3.100% Arabica or a Blend?
Most high-quality espresso beans are made from 100% Arabica, which offers nuanced flavors and a smoother cup. However, adding a small amount of Robusta (often 10–30%) can increase crema, body, and caffeine.
### 4.Single-Origin vs. Blends: Which is Better?
**Espresso Blends**
✔ Balanced flavor (often chocolate, nuts, spice)
✔ Consistent crema & body
✔ Easier to extract (great for beginners)
**Single-Origin (SOE)**
✔ Unique, terroir-driven flavors (e.g., berry, floral, citrus)
✔ Requires precise grind & extraction adjustments
✔ Best for experienced espresso lovers
### 5.Consider Your Grinder and Machine
Your espresso machine and grinder play a huge role in how beans perform. Entry-level grinders may struggle with consistency—especially for light roasts. High-end grinders (like those from [Wirsh Geimori series](https://mywirsh.com/pages/geimori)) offer stepless or micrometric adjustmentsto fine-tune every shot.
### 6.Grind Size & Consistency
Espresso requires a grind that's fine and uniform, almost like powdered sugar. Inconsistent or incorrect grind size leads to poor extraction.
Too coarse? Sour, under-extracted shot
Too fine? Bitter, over-extracted shot
### Final Thoughts
The right coffee beans are the foundation of great espresso. Whether you're after a rich chocolatey shot or a bright, fruity one, understanding bean origin, roast level, and freshness will help you make the most of your espresso machine.
