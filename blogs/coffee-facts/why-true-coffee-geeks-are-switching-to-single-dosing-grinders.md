Single-Dose Coffee Grinder Guide
A single-dose coffee grinder is designed to grind one measured portion of beans at a time instead of storing a larger quantity of coffee in the hopper. You weigh the beans, grind that dose, and repeat for the next brew.
**Quick answer** Single dosing is mainly a workflow choice. It makes bean switching, dose tracking, and storing most of your coffee outside the grinder easier. The trade-off is that you weigh and load beans for every brew, and low retention still matters.

## What Does "Single Dose" Mean?
With a traditional hopper workflow, you may pour a larger amount of beans into the grinder and leave them there for multiple drinks. With single dosing, the hopper or bean chamber starts essentially empty before each dose.
A typical routine looks like this:
1. Weigh the amount of coffee you want to brew.
1. Add that dose to the grinder.
1. Grind the full dose.
1. Use a bellows or other clearing step if the grinder is designed for it.
1. Repeat with the next coffee or recipe.
Single dosing does not automatically mean "zero retention," and it does not automatically make coffee taste better. Its main advantage is control over how much coffee enters the grinder and when.
## How Is a Single-Dose Grinder Different From a Hopper Grinder?
| Workflow | Single dose | Hopper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bean storage | Most beans stay outside the grinder | Multiple doses can stay in the hopper |
| Dose preparation | Weigh before each brew | Often grind from a stored supply |
| Switching beans | Usually easier | May require emptying or purging |
| Speed for many drinks | More manual steps | Usually faster for repeated drinks |
| Retention sensitivity | Especially important when switching coffees | Often less visible during repeated use of the same beans |

## What Are the Advantages of Single Dosing?
Advantage
### Switch beans more easily
Move from espresso to filter, regular to decaf, or one origin to another without first emptying a large hopper.
Advantage
### Know the dose going in
Weighing before grinding makes the input dose visible and repeatable, which can be useful when dialing in a recipe.
Advantage
### Store beans separately
Most of the bag can stay in its main storage container instead of sitting in the grinder between brews.
Advantage
### Less purging when changing coffee
A low-retention single-dose design can reduce how much coffee needs to be discarded when moving between beans or recipes.
## What Are the Downsides of Single Dosing?
Single dosing is not automatically the better workflow for every home barista.
Trade-off
### You weigh every dose
If you want to make several drinks quickly, repeatedly weighing and loading beans can feel slower than using a hopper.
Trade-off
### Retention still exists
Single-dose design can reduce retention, but coffee can still remain in the burr chamber or chute depending on the grinder and conditions.
Trade-off
### Bellows add another step
Some grinders use bellows to help clear retained grounds. Some users like the control; others prefer a simpler automatic routine.
Trade-off
### Bean feeding can vary
Very small hoppers and open single-dose chambers can behave differently with different bean sizes, roast levels, and feed designs.
## Is Single Dosing Actually Better?
Not automatically. It is better only when the workflow matches what you care about.
If you use one coffee all week, make several milk drinks each morning, and value speed above bean switching, a hopper can be extremely practical.
If you regularly change coffees, brew both espresso and filter, keep decaf available, or want to weigh every dose before grinding, single dosing may be more convenient despite the extra manual steps.
Single dosing is not a quality grade. It is a way of organizing the path from stored beans to one measured brew.
## Does Single Dosing Keep Coffee Fresher?
It can help because most of your coffee can stay outside the grinder until you are ready to brew. However, freshness depends on more than whether a grinder has a single-dose hopper.
Bean storage, oxygen exposure, temperature, humidity, roast age, and how far ahead you grind all matter. If the main goal is freshness, the most useful habit is often keeping beans stored well and grinding only the amount you need close to brewing.

## Why Retention Matters More in a Single-Dose Workflow
If you weigh 18 grams of one coffee and then immediately switch to another, coffee left inside the grinder can mix with the next dose. That is why retention and exchange matter when evaluating a single-dose grinder.
It is also possible for input and output weights to look similar while a small amount of older coffee is exchanged for newer coffee inside the grinder. Weight alone does not prove that the grinder is completely empty.

## What You'll Actually Notice
### You can switch coffee without emptying a full hopper
This is one of the most obvious day-to-day benefits for users who rotate beans or keep both regular and decaf coffee.
### You know how much coffee you started with
Weighing beans before grinding gives you a clear input dose and makes recipe changes easier to track.
### You add a few steps to every brew
Weighing, loading, and sometimes using bellows becomes part of the routine. Some people enjoy that ritual; others prefer speed.
### You start paying more attention to retention
Because each dose is treated separately, leftover grounds and bean exchange become easier to notice than they are in a hopper that runs the same coffee repeatedly.
## Who Is a Single-Dose Grinder Best For?
- Home baristas who switch between different coffees
- People who keep regular and decaf beans at the same time
- Users who brew both espresso and filter coffee
- People who like weighing and adjusting each recipe
- Users who want most beans stored outside the grinder
- Small households that make one or two drinks at a time
## Who Might Prefer a Hopper?
- Households making several drinks back-to-back
- Users who drink the same coffee for long stretches
- People who value speed and convenience over bean switching
- Users who do not want to weigh every dose manually
## If You Want a Single-Dose Grinder, What Should You Look For?
Do not judge a single-dose grinder only by the size of its hopper. Look at the whole workflow.
| Feature | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Adjustment system | Espresso users may need very small grind changes, while multi-method users need enough overall range. |
| Retention and exchange | Old grounds can mix into the next dose, especially when switching coffees. |
| Burr design | Burr geometry and the complete grinder design influence grinding behavior more than a single headline number. |
| Static control | Static can affect mess, grounds movement, and workflow. |
| Bellows / clearing design | Some users value the ability to clear the chute manually; others prefer fewer steps. |
| Counter space | A grinder can be technically excellent and still be wrong for your kitchen. |

## Single-Dose Grinder FAQ
### What is the point of a single-dose grinder?
The main point is to grind one measured dose at a time. This makes it easier to switch beans, track input dose, and keep most coffee stored outside the grinder.
### Do single-dose grinders have zero retention?
No grinder should be assumed to have literally zero retention. Low-retention designs aim to minimize coffee left inside, but actual retention depends on grinder design, coffee, grind size, static, humidity, cleaning, and technique.
### Is single dosing better for espresso?
It can be useful for espresso because users often weigh doses carefully and change grind settings while dialing in. It is not required for making good espresso.
### Do I need to weigh beans every time?
In a true single-dose workflow, yes. Weighing each portion before grinding is what makes the dose visible and repeatable.
### Is a single-dose grinder good for multiple brew methods?
It can be, especially if the grinder has enough adjustment range. Single dosing also makes it easier to switch between coffees used for espresso, pour-over, or other brew methods.
### Is a hopper grinder worse?
No. Hopper grinders can be faster and more convenient when you use the same coffee repeatedly or make several drinks in a row. The better choice depends on workflow.
## The Bottom Line
A single-dose coffee grinder is designed around one measured portion at a time. Its biggest strengths are flexibility, easier bean switching, and visibility into each dose. Its biggest trade-off is a more manual routine.
If that workflow matches the way you brew, single dosing can make your coffee station easier to control. If speed and repeated drinks with the same beans matter more, a hopper may be the better tool.
