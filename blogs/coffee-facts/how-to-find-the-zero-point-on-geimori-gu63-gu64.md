The GU63 and GU64 Gen2 use different zero-point calibration procedures.
**Direct answer:** A grinder's zero point is a repeatable reference for its adjustment scale, but the reference is established differently on the GU63 and GU64 Gen2. On the GU63, find the natural tightest position with the motor off, then rotate back to the marked `0`. On the GU64 Gen2, use the internal adjustment wheel to find the first light burr-contact reference, then re-index the external dial so the final displayed `0` sits away from direct burr contact.
Choose the correct procedure
## GU63 and GU64 Gen2 do not calibrate the same way.
Confirm your grinder model before following any rotation direction or burr-contact step.

A reliable zero reference makes a stepless grinder easier to dial in. Once the scale is calibrated, you can record espresso and filter settings relative to the same starting point instead of treating another grinder's printed number as a universal grind size.
## Safety before you start
- Make sure there are no coffee beans in the grinder before calibration.
- Keep fingers and tools away from the burr chamber.
- Never force an adjustment beyond its natural range.
- For the **GU63**, the fine-limit check is performed with the motor fully stopped. Never switch on the grinder while the hopper is at its tightest position.
- For the **GU64 Gen2**, the motor intentionally runs during the burr-contact step. Touch only the adjustment control shown in the official calibration procedure.
- If you hear prolonged harsh scraping, notice unusual vibration, or the adjustment does not move normally, stop the grinder.
## What does “zero point” mean?
Three different references can be easy to confuse:
**Printed zero** The `0` printed on the grinder's adjustment scale.
**Mechanical reference** The fine-end reference used during calibration: the natural tightest position on GU63, or the first light burr-contact cue on GU64 Gen2.
**Calibrated zero** The final scale reference established after completing the correct model-specific procedure.
The mechanical reference and the final calibrated `0` are not necessarily the same physical position. The goal is to create a repeatable scale reference without using sustained burr contact as a normal operating condition.

## Check and reset the calibration zero
The GU63 is factory-calibrated. If the reference has shifted, use the powered-off hopper procedure described in the current user manual.
1
### Empty the grinder and stop the motor
Place the GU63 on a flat, stable surface and make sure there are no beans inside. If the grinder is powered, confirm that the motor is fully stopped before checking the zero reference. Remove the hopper lid so you can hold the hopper securely.
2
### Turn counterclockwise to the natural tightest position
With the motor off, slowly rotate the hopper **counterclockwise** toward the fine end. Continue until you reach the natural tightest position. The GU63 manual notes that this point may sit slightly past the printed `0`.
**Stop at the natural limit. Do not force the hopper farther.**
![Geimori GU63 hopper turning counterclockwise toward the natural tightest position with the motor off](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0569/8247/0796/files/GU63_find_natural_finest_limit.webp?v=1789893464)
GU63: find the natural tightest position with the motor off.
**Important:** Do not switch on the GU63 while the hopper is at its tightest setting. The user manual warns that burr contact at this position can damage the burrs.
3
### Rotate clockwise back to the marked 0
From the tightest position, rotate the hopper **clockwise** until the printed `0` aligns with the grinder's reference mark. The GU63 manual defines this position as the calibration zero point, and the grinder is then ready to be adjusted for brewing.
![Geimori GU63 hopper returned clockwise so the printed zero aligns with the reference mark](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0569/8247/0796/files/GU63_return_to_marked_zero.webp?v=1789893078)
GU63: tightest mechanical position → clockwise → marked `0`.
**Keep the two positions separate:** the tightest mechanical position is used to establish the reference. The marked `0` is the calibrated scale position you return to afterward.

## Find the contact reference, then re-index the dial
The GU64 Gen2 calibration procedure uses the internal adjustment wheel to establish the mechanical reference before the outer dial is repositioned.
Phase 1 · Find the mechanical reference
1
### Remove the external dial knob
Make sure the grinder is empty. Remove the external adjustment knob to expose the internal adjustment wheel shown in the official calibration video.
2
### Turn the internal wheel counterclockwise
With the motor off, rotate the exposed internal adjustment wheel **counterclockwise by approximately two-thirds of a full turn**. Move smoothly and do not force the mechanism.
3
### Switch on the empty grinder
With the external knob still removed and no coffee in the grinder, switch on the GU64 Gen2. Keep hands and tools away from the burr chamber.
4
### Turn clockwise slowly until the first light burr-contact sound
With the motor running, slowly rotate the internal adjustment wheel **clockwise**. As soon as you hear the first light metallic burr-contact or scraping sound, **stop turning immediately**.
Do not continue moving finer and do not leave the burrs running in sustained contact. The sound is a calibration cue, not a normal grinding condition.
Phase 2 · Re-index the external scale
5
### Reinstall the dial knob at temporary 0
At the burr-contact reference, reinstall the external dial knob and align its reference line with `0`. At this point, the displayed zero is only a temporary indexing reference.
6
### Turn the dial until the indicator reaches setting 10
Rotate the dial until the grinder's indicator aligns with `10`. This moves the adjustment away from the burr-contact reference before the final zero is set.
7
### Remove the knob without changing the internal position
Remove the external knob again while keeping the internal adjustment position unchanged.
8
### Reinstall the knob with 0 at the indicator
Reposition the external knob so that `0` aligns with the grinder's indicator. The GU64 Gen2 zero-position calibration is now complete.
![Geimori GU64 Gen2 dial sequence showing temporary zero, setting 10 and the final re-indexed zero](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0569/8247/0796/files/GU64_reindex_dial_to_final_zero.webp?v=1789893079)
GU64 Gen2: burr-contact reference → temporary `0` → setting `10` → final re-indexed `0`.
**Why the extra re-indexing step matters:** the first burr-contact position is the mechanical reference. The final displayed `0` is established only after the outer dial is repositioned.
## GU63 vs. GU64 Gen2: the key difference
| Calibration point | GU63 | GU64 Gen2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main adjustment used | Hopper / upper adjustment assembly | External dial knob + internal adjustment wheel |
| Motor during mechanical-reference check | OFF | ON during the burr-contact step |
| Mechanical reference | Natural tightest position | First light burr-contact sound |
| Final calibrated zero | Rotate back to printed 0 | Re-index external dial after finding contact |
## When should you check the zero point?
Zero calibration is a reference procedure, not part of everyday dialing in. The GU63 is factory-calibrated, so you do not need to repeat the procedure when the scale remains correct. Rechecking the reference can be useful when the displayed zero no longer matches the expected position or after adjustment components have been removed and reinstalled.
## Use calibration to make your settings repeatable
Once the zero reference is established, use your grinder's scale as a repeatable reference for your own coffee and brewing setup. Instead of assuming that one printed number must work on every grinder, record what worked on your calibrated unit.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is zero the same as burr contact?
No. A mechanical contact or fine-limit position is used to establish calibration. The final calibrated `0` is the scale reference produced after completing the model-specific procedure.
### Can I grind at setting 0?
Do not confuse a calibrated `0` with direct burr contact. On the GU63, the official starting chart includes `0–20` as the espresso range after calibration. On the GU64 Gen2, the final zero is established only after the dial has been re-indexed away from the burr-contact reference.
### Should I make the GU63 burrs chirp?
No. The GU63 fine-limit check is performed with the motor off, and the manual warns not to switch the grinder on while the hopper is at its tightest position.
### Why does the GU64 Gen2 calibration use a burr-contact sound?
The first light burr-contact sound is used as the mechanical reference in the GU64 Gen2 calibration procedure. Once it is found, the external dial is re-indexed so the final displayed zero is separated from that contact point.
### Can I use the GU63 calibration method on the GU64 Gen2?
No. The two grinders use different adjustment mechanisms and different calibration procedures. Always follow the instructions for your exact model.
