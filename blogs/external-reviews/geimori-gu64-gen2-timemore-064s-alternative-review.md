## 1. Easier dialing: the clearest advantage in the review
A grinder can make small adjustments possible on paper and still feel awkward when you are chasing the right shot. The presenter’s strongest comparison addresses exactly that problem.
At [12:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aaVJjuVU3Ek&t=762s), they describe their Timemore as sensitive to very small dial movements: barely touching the adjustment can move the grind further than intended. The GU64, in their experience, makes those fine changes easier to manage.
The presenter returns to the point in the final assessment: **“the way it adjusts is considerably better than the Timemore.”** This is an English translation of the German assessment at [18:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aaVJjuVU3Ek&t=1128s).
That comparison comes from familiarity. Earlier in the video, the presenter explains that their first-generation Timemore had been used in the course setting for more than two years. They are assessing the GU64 against a grinder already embedded in their routine.
There is also a concrete adjustment reference: approximately **5.5 μm of burr movement per marked increment**, cited at [10:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aaVJjuVU3Ek&t=646s). That figure describes the mechanism’s movement, not the size of the coffee particles. For the buyer, the useful combination is fine mechanical adjustment and a reviewer who finds the control comfortable in practice.
When an espresso is almost where you want it, being able to make a small, deliberate correction matters. This is where the GU64’s strongest usability argument sits.

## 2. Better value: a roughly €200 gap, with usable control
The presenter quotes **about €360 for the GU64**, placing it **roughly €200 below Timemore** in the review’s price comparison. These are the figures stated in the video published on October 23, 2026; current regional pricing and configurations should be checked separately.
The saving is persuasive because it accompanies a positive assessment of the adjustment and the coffee. The reviewer is describing a lower-priced grinder whose controls they prefer in an important part of the espresso routine.
That gives an espresso buyer a practical reason to compare configurations carefully. If the GU64 offers the burr option and local support you need, its value proposition rests on both the purchase cost and how it feels to use every day.
Compare the exact grinder, burr configuration and daily experience you want. The review gives the GU64 a strong starting position: a lower quoted cost, preferred fine control and an enjoyable cup.

## 3. Espresso earns the recommendation: sweetness, body and fruit
The cup is where the review becomes most enthusiastic.
The first attempt runs finer than intended and produces a ristretto. The presenter tastes it anyway and praises its sweetness and body, describing no astringency in that cup. They then adjust coarser and try again.
For the later extraction, the video reports **18 g of coffee in and approximately 49 g of beverage across the two cups**—a brew ratio of about **1:2.7**. The presenter would normally prefer a more concentrated result, yet still praises the sweetness, body and berry-like fruit character.
That detail makes the tasting account useful. The viewer gets the dose, the yield and the presenter’s preference, alongside the reaction in the cup. Across both attempts, the same attractive qualities keep coming through.
At [17:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aaVJjuVU3Ek&t=1054s), the presenter concludes that the grinder can make **“excellent espresso”**—translated from the German. It is a strong endorsement of the coffee they made with the GU64.
For someone buying primarily for home espresso, that completes the argument: approachable fine adjustment, a lower quoted purchase price and a cup that earns an enthusiastic response from the reviewer.
**Choose the GU64 Gen2 for the combination these three points describe.** The review makes it a compelling option for an espresso-focused buyer who wants fine control and value, with convincing positive feedback on the coffee itself.
