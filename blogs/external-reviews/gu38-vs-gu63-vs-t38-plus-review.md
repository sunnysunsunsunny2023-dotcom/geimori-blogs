## What the burr difference means—and what it doesn’t
One hardware difference is immediately clear. The GU38 and T38 Plus use 38 mm six-core conical burrs, while the GU63 moves to a 63 mm eight-core conical platform.
That is useful information, but burr diameter alone should not decide the purchase. The more practical question is what comes with each platform: adjustment range, dose capacity, grinding rate, physical size and how the grinder fits into a daily routine.
The current GU63 product page lists a 100 W DC motor at 80 RPM, a marked 0–85 stepless adjustment range, a 60 g hopper and a 70 g dosing cup. The GU38 and T38 Plus use smaller 38 mm conical-burr platforms in more compact formats.
**These are measurable design differences—not proof that one grinder will taste better with every coffee.** Coffee, roast level, recipe, calibration and personal taste all influence what ends up in the cup.

### T38 Plus: compact plug-in grinding
The T38 Plus is the smallest and lightest of these three. Its 234 mm height and 1,234 g weight suit a small counter, office station or setup that moves regularly. It is plug-in powered, so portable means compact and easy to relocate—not cordless.
### GU38: a slim everyday grinder
The GU38 is a taller, heavier single-dose grinder with a slim 3.5-inch body. The current page positions it for filter-first users and occasional espresso, with 38 mm six-core conical burrs, stepless adjustment and a low-retention workflow.
### GU63: the larger conical-burr platform
The GU63 combines 63 mm eight-core conical burrs, an 80 RPM motor, a 60 g hopper, a 70 g dosing cup and a 0–85 adjustment scale. The trade-off is physical: 3,068 g and a 305 mm height take more counter space and make moving the grinder less convenient than the smaller T38 Plus.
## One thing the video cannot decide for you: taste
A hands-on comparison can show size, workflow, construction and practical differences. It cannot automatically tell you which grinder will produce the cup profile *you* prefer.
Coffee drinkers may prioritize very different things: body and texture, clarity and flavor separation, traditional espresso, lighter-roast espresso, filter coffee, milk drinks or versatility across several brewing methods.
That is why this review is more useful as a decision aid than as a universal ranking. Use Dimitris’s real-world observations to understand the machines, use the current product pages to verify the specifications, and then bring the decision back to the coffee you actually drink.

## Frequently asked questions
### Are the GU38 and T38 Plus the same grinder?
They share the same listed 38 mm six-core conical-burr platform and 170 RPM motor, but their bodies, dimensions, base construction and workflow differ. Treat them as related models, not interchangeable products.
### Is the GU63 faster for espresso?
The current GU63 product page lists 0.6 g/s for espresso, the same listed rate as the GU38 and T38 Plus. Its advantages are the larger burr platform, larger capacity and filter-rate figure—not a promised espresso speed advantage.
### Is the T38 Plus cordless?
No. The T38 Plus uses a plug-in AC–DC adapter. For cordless brewing, compare the battery-powered T38.
### Do the grinders work with different mains voltages?
The current product pages list 100–240 V, 50/60 Hz adapter input. A region-matched plug head may still be required for the outlet in your destination.
