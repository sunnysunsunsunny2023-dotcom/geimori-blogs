## What was actually compared?
Espresso Hackers realigned his DF64 with SSP MP burrs before comparing V60 and espresso using light-roast Ethiopia and medium-dark Gesha.
The important distinction for a buyer is the configuration: this is a comparison against a DF64 with an aftermarket burr upgrade. Keep that complete setup in mind when weighing the results and the cost. It would be misleading to turn this into a conclusion about every DF64 configuration.
## 1. V60 with light-roast Ethiopia: a small flavor advantage
**His observations:** Hot cups were close. Around 50°C, GU63 tasted juicier; DF64 was flatter and slightly more bitter. Around 37°C, GU63 retained a tiny advantage. He doubted he could identify them blind.
**What this means for your shortlist:** If you enjoy a lively cup, pay attention to that preference for juiciness. The useful buying signal is that GU63 deserved consideration on flavor, even beside an upgraded setup. A modest preference can still matter when it matches the style of coffee you look forward to each morning.
Keep the scale of the result in proportion. This is a reason to consider GU63 confidently, rather than a reason to expect a dramatic difference with every Ethiopian coffee.
## 2. V60 with medium-dark Gesha: fruit expression stands out
**His observations:** Both cups were floral. GU63 was juicier at 60°C and remained fruitier at 50°C; DF64’s notes seemed more muted. He preferred GU63.
**What this means for your shortlist:** Think about which quality you want to bring forward in your cup. Someone who seeks expressive fruit may place more weight on this result than someone whose priority is a different flavor balance.
That gives the GU63 an appeal beyond its price tag. For a pour-over drinker, enjoying the way a grinder presents a coffee is a reason to choose it in its own right.

## 3. Ethiopian espresso: a closer look as the cups cool
**His observations:** DF64 tasted clearer and more intense. GU63’s shot ran three seconds faster. Cooling narrowed the gap.
**What to take from this:** The reviewer preferred the upgraded DF64 for this espresso. Yet the narrowing gap as the cups cooled puts that preference in perspective: this result does not turn the GU63 into a pour-over-only proposition. For a home brewer, the relevant question is how much a particular difference matters across the drinks they make every day.
If pour-over is a regular part of your routine, weigh the whole comparison. The GU63’s appeal lies in the combination of its filter-coffee performance and the opportunity to explore espresso within the same budget.
## 4. Gesha espresso: clarity and balance are different priorities
**His observations:** GU63 tasted fruitier, more acidic and clearer; DF64 was more balanced and his preference. GU63’s shot ran two seconds faster.
**What this means for your shortlist:** “Clearer” and “more balanced” answer different questions. When reading tasting notes, consider both how distinctly you want to perceive flavors and how you want those flavors to fit together. Choosing a favorite involves weighing those qualities.
For drinkers who enjoy a fruit-forward espresso, the GU63’s character is worth attention. The reviewer’s preference for balance does not make fruit expression or clarity less desirable; it shows how personal the final choice can be.
## How much can these results tell us?
A close tasting comparison is most useful as a guide to what to explore. The shot-time differences are a reason to avoid attributing every sensory difference solely to the grinders. They also do not establish that another adjustment would reverse the result.
This video does not establish a comparative ranking for retention, noise, grinding speed, cleaning, or durability. Those would require separate evidence. We also would not describe the tasting as a confirmed blind-test victory.
