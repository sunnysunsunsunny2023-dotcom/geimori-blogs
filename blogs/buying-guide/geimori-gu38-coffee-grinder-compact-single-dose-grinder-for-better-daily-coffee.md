## 38 mm Conical Burr: What It Actually Means
GU38 uses 38 mm 6-core conical burrs. The practical point is not that 38 mm is a magic number. Burr diameter is only one part of a grinder's design.
For GU38, the relevant combination is the compact conical burr system, 170 RPM motor speed, stepless adjustment, and slim housing. Together, those choices define the grinder's role as a smaller home single-dose option.
Burr size matters, but it should be judged together with adjustment, alignment, motor behavior, retention, and the coffee you actually brew.
## Stepless Adjustment: Probably GU38's Most Important Upgrade
One of the most frustrating problems with basic stepped grinders is the "in-between" setting: one click runs too fast for espresso, while the next click runs too slow.
Stepless adjustment lets you make smaller changes between those fixed positions. That does not guarantee better espresso by itself, but it gives you more control when dialing in.
This is especially useful when you want to keep dose and yield stable and use grind size as the main variable.

## Low Retention and the Single-Dose Workflow
Single dosing means weighing a dose before grinding rather than filling a large hopper with several drinks' worth of beans. For this workflow, retention becomes important because coffee left in the chamber can mix with the next dose.
GU38 is designed around a low-retention path. Geimori currently specifies less than 0.1 g retention on a 10 g dose when the bellows are used, but real-world retention can vary with coffee, grind size, static, humidity, seasoning, and technique.
It is also worth separating retention from exchange. A grinder can return roughly the same weight you put in while still exchanging a small amount of older coffee with the new dose.

## What You'll Actually Notice in Daily Use
### Smaller changes when dialing in espresso
The practical benefit of stepless adjustment is having room between fixed clicks. That makes it easier to make a small correction when a shot is close but not quite where you want it.
### Less coffee left between doses
A low-retention workflow is useful when switching beans, moving between regular and decaf, or simply trying to keep one dose from mixing heavily with the next.
### A grinder that does not dominate the counter
GU38's body is about 90 mm wide. For a small coffee station, that narrow footprint can matter just as much as a specification on the burr or motor.

## GU38 Buyer FAQ
### Is GU38 good for espresso?
GU38 is designed to grind fine enough for espresso and uses stepless adjustment, which is useful when dialing in. Espresso results still depend on coffee, dose, puck preparation, machine, recipe, and grind setting.
### Can GU38 grind for pour-over?
Yes. GU38 is designed as an all-purpose home grinder covering espresso through filter coffee, so it can move into coarser settings for pour-over and other brew methods.
### Is GU38 a single-dose grinder?
Yes. Its workflow is designed around weighing a dose before grinding rather than storing a large supply of beans in the grinder.
### Is a 38 mm burr enough for home espresso?
Burr diameter alone does not determine espresso quality. For home use, adjustment resolution, burr geometry, alignment, motor behavior, retention, coffee, and technique all matter alongside burr size.
### What is the main difference between GU38 and GU63?
GU38 prioritizes a slimmer, lower-cost home setup with 38 mm conical burrs and 170 RPM grinding. GU63 moves to a larger 63 mm conical burr system, 80 RPM operation, and a more advanced countertop workflow.
### Who should choose T38 Plus instead?
T38 Plus makes more sense when portability and a smaller plug-in form factor are higher priorities than using a dedicated countertop grinder.
