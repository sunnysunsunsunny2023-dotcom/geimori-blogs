Geimori Engineering Notes / 64mm Flat Burrs
**SSP burrs in a GU64 Gen2?** It's a fair question. The GU64 uses 64mm flat burrs and has a quick-access burr design. But having the same outside diameter doesn't mean an aftermarket burr set will automatically mount correctly or run safely.
We've seen the questions from people who already own SSP burrs, as well as people who want a grinder they can experiment with over time. So here's where things stand, what still needs checking, and why the answer may involve a custom burr carrier rather than just a new set of screws.

## A Reddit burr swap that got us thinking
In a recent [r/pourover discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/pourover/comments/1x14w56/starseeker_e64_ssp_mp/), an owner swapped the stock burrs on a Starseeker E64 for inexpensive, SSP MP-style burrs. After aligning them, they reported clearer filter brews and better flavor separation. They were especially pleased with how the change softened an overly strong fermented note in one familiar coffee.
The distinction matters: **those were third-party SSP MP-style burrs, not genuine SSP burrs.** The owner had only made a handful of brews and hadn't compared them against real SSPs. Some commenters had very different experiences with similar-looking burrs. It's an interesting owner report, not controlled testing or a reason to assume every MP-shaped burr tastes alike.
What we took from it was the question underneath the discussion: what if you like your grinder's workflow but want to try a different burr geometry, instead of buying a completely different grinder?

## Why people ask about SSP
SSP makes several 64mm burr designs that appeal to different brewing preferences. Multi-Purpose (MP) burrs are often chosen by people chasing clarity and flavor separation, while other SSP geometries may be selected for different espresso or filter priorities. Even within the 64mm category, names, versions and intended uses matter.
And it's worth separating three ideas: an SSP-branded burr set, a non-genuine SSP-style burr set, and the Geimori stock MP burrs are **not the same product**. Similar-looking teeth do not establish identical geometry, tolerances or cup performance.

## So, do SSP burrs fit the GU64 Gen2?
**Short answer:** We cannot currently promise universal drop-in compatibility between the GU64 Gen2's stock mounting hardware and every genuine SSP 64mm burr variant. If you're planning an SSP conversion, please contact us with the exact burr model before buying parts or attempting installation.
GU64 Gen2 has 64mm flat burrs, stepless adjustment, a quick-swap access mechanism and adjustable [800-1200](tel:800-1200) RPM driven by a BLDC motor. These features make it a useful platform to explore burr choice, but they do not establish third-party fitment by themselves.
Compatibility depends on both sides of the assembly: the burrs and the parts holding them. A set can have the right outside diameter and still fail to seat correctly, leave insufficient clearance, or fail to run concentrically with the grinder's axis.

## It's not just a 64mm measurement
Before an aftermarket burr can be called compatible, several mechanical details need to agree:
| What needs to match | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Center bore and locating pilot | The burr needs to locate securely and concentrically on its carrier, without interference or excess play. |
| Screw pattern and hole geometry | Hole centers, screw size, countersink/recess and the carrier's threads must suit the burr design. |
| Burr thickness and seating faces | Changes in stack height can alter zero-point position and internal clearance. |
| Fixed and rotating carrier interfaces | Both burrs must mount flat and remain aligned under normal operating loads. |
| Cutting geometry, rotation and clearance | The burr set must work with the grinder's rotation direction, feed path and available chamber space. |
This is also why we don't recommend pressing a tight-fitting burr into place, drilling a burr, modifying mounting holes, or assuming that an installation is safe because all three screws can be started.

## Could a custom burr carrier solve the problem?
**Potentially, yes.** The practical route may be a purpose-machined burr carrier or mounting hub designed around the exact SSP burr variant and the GU64's existing drive interface. Depending on the geometry, an adapter or shaft-coupled mounting part could also be involved.
That is different from immediately replacing or machining the motor shaft itself. The motor shaft, rotating carrier, stationary seat and connecting parts have different jobs. Our engineering team would first need to identify which interface is creating the fitment problem. Only then could we decide whether a carrier change is enough or whether a more substantial design change is required.
### What information would we need?
- The **exact SSP model and version** you're hoping to use (for example, MP, HU or a specific Lab Sweet version), plus photographs of the actual set.
- An **official dimensional drawing or a physical burr sample** so we can measure the bore, mounting holes, thickness and seating surfaces rather than guessing from the 64mm label.
- The **GU64 production version** and measurements of the stock rotating and stationary mounting interfaces.
- A bench validation plan covering fit, flat seating, burr alignment/runout, clearance, recalibration and grinding tests with representative coffees.
Even with drawings, we would want to test the actual parts before treating a conversion as approved. A CAD drawing can tell us if something should fit. It cannot, on its own, tell us whether the installed assembly is aligned well enough to grind as intended.
## What about burr alignment and recalibration?
A burr swap is more than an exchange of cutting surfaces. After changing burrs, the grinder's contact point and usable grind range can move. That doesn't automatically indicate a problem; it does mean you need to check the installation and recalibrate according to a validated procedure for that burr and grinder combination.
Fellow's [official SSP installation guidance for its Ode grinder](https://help.fellowproducts.com/hc/en-us/articles/360059153572-How-do-I-install-and-calibrate-SSP-burrs-in-my-Ode-Brew-Grinder) is a useful reminder that even a manufacturer-supported burr swap has model-specific steps and limits. Those instructions are for the Ode, *not* for the GU64. Please don't copy them over to another grinder.

## Do you actually need SSP burrs?
Maybe, maybe not. GU64 already offers Geimori Multi-Purpose (MP) and Filter (F) burr configurations. For someone who mostly pulls espresso but makes the occasional V60, the MP set may already be a good starting point. For someone who primarily drinks pour-over, the F option is worth considering before spending on a third-party conversion.
We've written a separate [GU64 MP vs F burr guide](https://mywirsh.com/blogs/buying-guide/geimori-gu64-mp-burr-vs-f-burr-one-grinder-two-different-cup-styles) for people trying to choose between our supported configurations. ## Where we stand today
We've heard the requests for wider 64mm aftermarket support, especially SSP. We also know our earlier discussions about third-party burr possibilities left some owners expecting broader compatibility than we can currently confirm. We should be clear about that.
Our approach is to evaluate specific burr designs and, where technically feasible, investigate a matching carrier or mounting solution. **That is a development and fitment question, not an announcement of a universally compatible SSP kit or a release date.**
When we have a verified solution, we'd rather publish the supported burr versions and installation limits than leave people guessing.
