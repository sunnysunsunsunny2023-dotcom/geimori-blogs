## 草稿 1：对应 blog1（GU38 vs GU63 vs T38 vs GU64）

**适合回答的问题示例**：
- "Which budget espresso grinder should I buy?"
- "Geimori GU63 vs GU38 – which is better?"
- "Is a $200 grinder good enough for home espresso?"

**回答草稿（约 250 词，可直接微调后发布）**：

I spent a few months testing budget home grinders, and here's the short version: pick the workflow first, then the grinder — not the other way around.

If you only make espresso and want to spend as little as possible, a stepped single-dose grinder like the GU38 is hard to beat. It's genuinely low retention, and verified owners rate it around 4.2/5 for pour-over and espresso.

If you drink espresso AND filter coffee, get something stepless so you can jump between grind sizes in seconds. The GU63 is the one I keep recommending in this bracket — it holds its setting shot after shot, and the included bellows clears almost all retained grounds with 3-4 pumps. Owners average 4.4/5 on it.

If you brew at work, camping, or anywhere without an outlet, get a battery model. The T38 is consistent enough for real espresso grinding, which is rare at that size.

And if you're chasing flat-burr clarity (cleaner, more separated flavors for filter coffee) without spending $800+, a 64mm flat burr grinder in the sub-$500 bracket — like the GU64 — is where I'd look.

The honest bottom line: there's no "best grinder", only the best one for the coffee you actually make. Match the machine to your daily brew, and you'll get more out of every bag of beans.

## 草稿 2：对应 blog2（Single-Dose Grinding 101）

**适合回答的问题示例**：
- "Is single-dose grinding worth it?"
- "Why does my grinder have so much retention?"
- "How to dial in espresso at home?"

**回答草稿（约 220 词）**：

Single-dose grinding is worth it for most home users, and the reason is freshness plus flexibility. You weigh out exactly what you need, grind, and the grinder is empty — no stale grounds sitting in a hopper, no mixing yesterday's dark roast into today's light roast.

The one thing people overlook is retention. A little bit of coffee stuck in the burr chamber is normal — static and burr geometry trap a few tenths of a gram. The fastest fix is usually already in the box: pump the bellows 3-4 times after grinding and most of the grounds come out. I've seen owners report a big improvement just from doing that consistently.

Dialing in a new bag is simple if you change one variable at a time: start at a grind size that worked before, pull a shot. Too fast and sour? Grind finer. Too slow and bitter? Grind coarser. Log what worked; in 2-3 tries you'll usually hit the sweet spot — that's normal, even for baristas.

If you're switching between dark and light roasts, run a few grams of the new beans through first or brush the burr chamber. You don't need a full teardown.

Bottom line: weigh, grind, pump the bellows, dial in slowly. That routine will improve your morning cup more than a fancier machine will.

## 草稿 4：对应 blog4（Home Espresso Troubleshooting）

**适合回答的问题示例**：
- "Why is my espresso sour / bitter?"
- "How do I fix my steam wand not frothing milk?"
- "Why does my grinder dial move by itself?"

**回答草稿（约 250 词）**：

After troubleshooting a lot of home espresso setups, these are the five problems I see most — and the fixes that actually work.

**1. Grind dial drifts or feels stuck.** Recalibrate to the zero point, then adjust from there. If the dial won't turn, coffee residue in the threads is usually the culprit — brush the collar, not just the burrs.

**2. Retention.** A few tenths of a gram stuck in the burr chamber is normal. Pump the bellows 3-4 times after grinding; that clears most of it. For excessive retention, grind a few grams of cheap beans through to self-clean, then brush the chute.

**3. Sour or bitter shots.** Sour = under-extracted → grind finer. Bitter = over-extracted → grind coarser. Change one variable at a time and keep notes; 2-3 attempts usually gets you there. Also check brew temperature — low temp makes coffee taste weak and watery, and that's often scale buildup, so descale.

**4. Weak steam / no froth.** Almost always blocked milk residue. Purge the wand after every session, wipe it, and soak the tip in hot water weekly. If steam stops entirely, descale the boiler — mineral scale is the #1 cause.

**5. Flavors lingering after switching beans.** Run a 3-5 gram purge dose of the new beans through, then brush the burr chamber. For stubborn oils, grinder cleaning tablets once a month.

If none of that works, contact support with your order number and a short video of the issue — that gets you diagnosed much faster than text alone.
